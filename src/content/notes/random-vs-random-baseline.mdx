## Core concept

Before writing any agent that tries to win, run two uniform-random players
against each other over a batch of seeded deals. Neither side has any skill,
so the points margin the harness reports should be statistically
indistinguishable from zero. It's the cheapest check that the harness itself
isn't lying.

A measurement tool that hasn't been shown to report "no difference" when there
genuinely is none can't be trusted to report a real difference later. So
random vs random isn't a throwaway smoke test. It's the zero the whole harness
is calibrated against, before any claim about an agent is allowed to rest on
it.

## What it caught in Belote AI

I got `BeloteGame` and `BeloteState` far enough along in the OpenSpiel fork to
deal cards, enforce bidding and play legal tricks to a scored end, then wrote
the first match evaluator and ran random against random.

Getting the paired-seed dealing right took a couple of passes: both sides have
to see the same deals, so that the luck of the deck doesn't swamp the signal.
An early version shared one RNG stream across both players' decisions, which
subtly biased whoever acted first on tie-breaks.

The finished harness reports a tie, -0.04 [-3.52, +3.45] over 1,199 deals, on
the [Belote AI page](/projects/belote-ai/#simulation-harness).

## Rule of thumb

Before a harness compares two agents, make it compare two identical random
ones, and don't trust it until it reports a tie.
